The six principles

Six claims argue the shape of the machine. Everything else is derived.

01Humans decide, agents execute.Every stage boundary is a human-openable gate. The human's acts are decisions and delegations; the agent's acts are execution against them.
02The tracker is the system of record.Issues have state, and state transitions are the process. Every stage emits an artifact and the ticket links it — spec in the repo, long-form record at its home, decision records with the code — so no decision survives only in a session.
03Evidence gates every transition.Each gate names its evidence kind in advance — a spec's validation gate may accept a preview screenshot; Done's evidence is the canary. Advancing state without it is a process violation, not a shortcut.
04No AFK without a definition of done.Work leaves human hands only when deliverable and acceptance criteria are settled. This is what the ready-for-agent route means; dispatching without it is a gamble, not a delegation.
05Sessions are sized to the smart zone.One ticket per implement session, fresh context each. Decisions travel between sessions by artifact, never by compaction.
06Attention is expensive.Every surface spends the reader's attention only where their judgement is needed: the glance carries the verdict, the long-form home carries the depth, one click between them. A ticket is never a wall of text, and a question answerable by lookup never reaches a human.
Principle three is the one that bites. A green test is worth nothing on its own. The evidence a gate accepts is named before the work starts, not argued about after it.
